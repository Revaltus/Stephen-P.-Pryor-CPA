## LinkedIn

A $300 difference in October is a five-minute question. The same difference in March is a mystery.

That gap is why contractors with messy books pay more at tax time. Picture a remodeling company with six accounts: operating checking, a job-deposit account, two credit cards, an equipment loan, and a line of credit. If none were reconciled, a preparer can spend a full day matching statements to the ledger before touching a single tax line. Do that same matching in November and a bookkeeper finishes in a couple of hours, because you still remember the charges.

The post covers the four Q4 cleanup jobs that matter most: reconcile every account, separate personal spending, fix miscategorized transactions (like a truck payment coded as materials), and collect subcontractor W-9s before the January 31 1099-NEC deadline.

If you run a construction company in Port Chester, Westchester, or Fairfield County, read it before December 31 and let your CPA spend January on tax planning instead of detective work.
