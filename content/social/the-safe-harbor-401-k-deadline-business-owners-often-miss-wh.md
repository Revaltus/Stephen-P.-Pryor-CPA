## LinkedIn

A contractor paying himself $250,000 can end up deferring only $10,000 into the 401(k), even though the 2025 limit is $23,500.

The reason is the crew. If field workers and apprentices average 2% deferrals, the owner group generally can't average more than 4%. That leaves $13,500 of your own contribution room stuck behind a test your crew's paychecks are controlling.

A safe harbor plan skips that test, but the timing is tight. A match plan needs an employee notice 30 to 90 days before the year starts. A 3% nonelective can generally be adopted until about 30 days before year-end, which is around December 1. After that, the same result costs 4%. On a sample $1,000,000 payroll, that is $30,000 versus $40,000.
