## Choosing the right entity shapes your taxes for years to come

The entity you pick when you start a business, or the one you inherited when you took over from a parent or partner, quietly controls a lot: what you owe the IRS every April, how exposed your personal assets are if something goes wrong, and how easily you can bring in investors or sell down the road. Most owners make this call once, early on, often based on a quick answer from a lawyer or a form they found online. That decision doesn't have to be permanent, and it doesn't have to be a guess.

Whether you're structuring a brand-new company or wondering if the LLC you set up eight years ago still fits, entity type analysis gives you a clear answer grounded in your numbers, not a generic template. The team at Stephen P. Pryor, CPA walks through the tradeoffs with you in plain language, so you understand the why behind the recommendation.

<!-- block: process-steps | variant: vertical -->
## How we evaluate your options

Entity selection isn't a single calculation. It's a handful of questions, answered honestly, that point toward sole proprietorship, partnership, S-corp, C-corp, or LLC.

### Understand your income goals

We start with where the business is headed financially: current revenue, projected growth, and how much of that income you plan to reinvest versus draw out personally.

### Assess your liability exposure

Some structures shield personal assets better than others. We look at your industry risk, contracts, and whether liability protection should outweigh simpler tax treatment.

### Map your growth plans

Bringing on partners, raising capital, or eventually selling the business all favor different entity types. We factor in where you want to be in five or ten years, not just today.

### Weigh state and federal tax implications

New York, Connecticut, New Jersey, and Florida each treat entity types differently for state tax purposes. We run the numbers across federal and state obligations before recommending a structure.

### Recommend a structure and explain the reasoning

You get a clear recommendation, in plain terms, along with the math behind it, so you can make the final call with confidence instead of guesswork.

## Support for construction, real estate, and hospitality businesses

Entity structure decisions rarely play out the same way twice. A contractor juggling three active job sites has different liability concerns than a bar owner handling cash receipts every night. We built our approach around the industries we know best.

### Construction

icon: Hammer

Job costing and bonding requirements often push contractors toward multi-entity structures, separating equipment ownership from operations to protect assets project by project. We help you weigh whether that separation makes sense given your project volume and crew size.

### Real Estate

icon: Home

Holding companies can separate liability from individual properties and make cost segregation and 1031 exchange planning cleaner down the line. We look at your portfolio size and acquisition plans before recommending how to structure each asset.

### Bars and Restaurants

icon: Receipt

Ownership structures for bars and pubs need to account for thin margins, tip reporting, and sales tax exposure across one location or several. We've worked through these questions with hospitality owners in across the tri-state area and beyond, and know where the tax traps usually sit.

## Revisiting your structure as your business grows

A lot of business owners set up their LLC or S-corp years ago, when revenue was smaller and the future looked different, and never looked at it again. That's normal. It's also often costly. As income climbs, an S-corp election can trim self-employment tax that a sole proprietorship or straight LLC leaves on the table. As liability exposure grows, a single-entity structure that made sense at $200,000 in revenue might leave too much exposed at $2 million.

We treat entity review as something to revisit periodically, not a one-time box to check. If your revenue, ownership, or risk profile has shifted since your last filing, it's worth twenty minutes to find out whether your current structure still fits or whether a change could mean real savings and better protection.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Entity Type Analysis

**Q: What is entity type analysis?**
A: Entity type analysis is a review of your business's income, liability exposure, and growth plans to determine whether a sole proprietorship, partnership, S-corp, C-corp, or LLC gives you the best tax and legal outcome. It's the foundation for both new business setup and ongoing tax strategy.

**Q: How do I know if my current business structure still fits?**
A: If your revenue, ownership, or risk exposure has changed significantly since you set up your entity, it's worth a review. Growth, new partners, or expansion into new markets can all shift which structure saves the most in taxes and offers the best liability protection.

**Q: Does entity choice affect taxes differently across states?**
A: Yes. New York, Connecticut, New Jersey, and Florida each apply different tax treatment to entity types, including franchise taxes and state-level pass-through rules.
